Offline Health Guides

First Aid: Skin Infections

Floodwaters contain harmful bacteria. Prompt care for cuts is vital.

  • Clean Immediately: Wash hands first. Wash the wound with clean, safe water (bottled or boiled) and soap.
  • Disinfect: Apply an antiseptic solution (like povidone-iodine) if available.
  • Cover: Cover with a clean, dry bandage or cloth. Keep it as dry as possible.
  • Monitor: Watch for redness, swelling, warmth, or pus. Seek medical help if these occur.
  • Avoid Floodwater: Do not let open wounds touch floodwater again.

Water Purification Methods

If bottled water is unavailable, you must purify water before drinking or cleaning wounds.

  • Boiling (Most Reliable)
    Filter cloudy water through a clean cloth. Bring to a rolling boil for at least 1 full minute (3 minutes at high altitudes). Let it cool naturally.
  • Chlorine Bleach
    Use regular, unscented household bleach (5.25%-8.25% chlorine). Add 2 drops per liter (or 8 drops per gallon) of clear water. Stir well and let stand for 30 minutes before using.
  • Solar Water Disinfection (SODIS)
    Fill clear plastic bottles (PET) with relatively clear water. Expose to direct sunlight for at least 6 hours (or 2 days if cloudy).
